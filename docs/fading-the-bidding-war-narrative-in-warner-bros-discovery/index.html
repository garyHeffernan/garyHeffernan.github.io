Fading the Bidding War Narrative in Warner Bros Discovery

× Date: Sun, Jan 4, 2026

Overview

Warner Bros. Discovery (”WBD”) entered a merger agreement with Netflix (”NFLX”) on December 4, 2025. The deal is a mix of cash and collared stock, equating to $27.75 for the streaming and studios business only (”WB S&S”) of WBD. The linear television company, Discovery Global Networks, is not included in the transaction.

Discovery Global Networks, or “DGN” will spin-out as a separate publicly traded company in 3Q 2026. A certain amount of debt is allocated to DGN from WB S&S.

If the DGN business deteriorates more than expected between now and the spin-off date, debt will be allocated to WB S&S (the company NFLX is buying) from DGN. Each dollar that is allocated to WB S&S reduces NFLX’s purchases price on a dollar for dollar basis, leaving today’s WBD shareholders exposed to a declining DGN business.

Concurrently with the exclusive negotiations that culminated in a merger agreement between Netflix and WBD, Paramount Skydance (”PSKY”) made a proposal to acquire the entirety of WBD for $30/share in cash. This bid was either rebuffed or not even considered, depending on whose story you believe.

Subsequently the board of WBD made it public that the PSKY bid was not superior to that of NFLX’s on a risk-adjusted basis. The board claimed that it required a personal guarantee from Larry Ellison, the controller and largest investor in PSKY, in order to consider the PSKY $30/share bid. Ellison delivered on the personal guarantee on December 22, 2025.

Leaks to the press since the personal guarantee suggest the WBD board still finds the PSKY bid inferior, and wants a higher price. Leaks, ostensibly from the PSKY side, suggest PSKY will not be raising their $30/sh bid. That brings us to today.

The events leading to today suggest an asymmetric risk/reward in shorting WBD if you believe the following:

  1. Sentiment Shift: Incessant press coverage has made what was once a completely ignored and unloved stock the subject of much speculation. Prominent security analysts and blog writers – those who had no interest in the stock when it was deeply discounted – are now positioned for a bidding war between PSKY and NFLX.

    • My contention is this sentiment is reflected in the WBD stock price.

    • If a bidding war were not to materialize then the shares would drop.

    Please see the (paid) link immediately below for an example of a prominent blog writer who became interested in WBD after it signed a merger agreement with NFLX, not before. He is one example of an investor positioned for a bidding war.

  1. Capped Upside: The upside in the stock is capped at around these prices if we do not enter a bidding war. The downside is tremendous in the case of a broken deal on account of:

    • a) Where WBD was trading before the merger announcement.

    • b) Hollywood studios are valued highly in control transactions, but absent that, trade poorly in the public markets (WBD’s recent history should make that point abundantly clear).

  2. The “Bid” Reality: There have been several press leaks over the last month that suggest PSKY is unwilling to move above their $30 price. There have been several press leaks over the last month that suggest WBD is unwilling to accept PSKY’s $30 bid.

    • Once it becomes clear that PSKY is not increasing their bid, the “bidding war premium” will evaporate from the share price.

    • This could bring the shares back down closer to the mid-20s which is where WBD traded after the NFLX merger announcement but before press leaks indicated that PSKY was not going away so easily.

  3. The Merger Arb / Antitrust Angle: What you’re really playing for if you’re short: WBD rejects PSKY’s $30 bid, PSKY thereafter announces that they’re withdrawing their bid. At that point, WBD’s stock price is determined by the likelihood that it can consummate the NFLX merger.

    My view is that the deal is unlikely to close as written if the government defines the relevant market as subscription streaming services.

    • The overlap between NFLX and HBO Max – combining the number 1 and the number 4 streaming services – would be an insurmountable hurdle for this deal.

    • The deal could go to court, which could cause an extended timeline to close (the drop dead date for the deal is in September 2027). Note, the timeline to close affects the trading price of WBD (i.e. the more time to close, the higher the spread, all else equal).

    • Again this has the effect of capping the risk to the short position and gives optionality for a break, upon which would send the shares sharply lower.

    The Regulatory Arguments:
    By PSKY’s estimates, Netflix would achieve a 43% share of global Subscription Video on Demand subscribers by acquiring WBD. This would result in higher streaming prices for consumers. As well, there is a monopsony argument to be made here:

    • WBD is a massive content supplier to other streamers; removing them from the marketplace would result in lower prices paid to the creative community for their work.
    • This line of argumentation was used successfully by the government in blocking the Penguin Random House / Simon & Schuster merger.

  4. The Variable Purchase Price: The deal structure implies a variable purchase price that is inversely correlated with the performance of the legacy linear business. The “Discovery Global Networks” (DGN) spin-off involves a complex debt allocation.

    • If DGN’s financial health deteriorates between now and the spin-off (3Q 2026), additional debt shifts from DGN to the entity Netflix is acquiring (WB S&S).

    • Shifting the debt reduces the purchase price received by today’s WBD investors, dollar-for-dollar.

    • Given the secular headwinds facing linear TV, this creates a tangible risk of the deal consideration shrinking below the headline $27.75 figure even if the deal closes.

    As well, the NFLX stock consideration involves a collar, and the NFLX stock price has already fallen below the floor of the collar. Using prices as of the last close, the Netflix stock consideration is ~30 cents below the headline deal price.

Where do the Shares Trade if the Deal Breaks?

WBD was trading at about $12 before the news broke that the Ellison family placed a hostile bid for the company (see image below). The shares closed Friday at $28.51 and, in my view, discount some probability of a bidding war taking place.

If the bidding war fails to materialize AND the Netflix deal ultimately breaks, there is a lot of daylight between the current price and where the shares are likely to stabilize.

Risks to the Short

Should the press leaks prove incorrect and the WBD board accepts PSKY’s bid of $30/share, I don’t see much risk to the short position, absent Netflix returning with a higher bid. Which leads me to my next point.

The biggest risk to a short position is that a bidding war does break out. Doubly bad would be a new entrant (think: big tech company) entering the bidding war, but I find that extremely unlikely.

Note: press leaks indicate WBD would walk away from Netflix if Ellison were to pay $34/share, or +$5.50 from last close.
