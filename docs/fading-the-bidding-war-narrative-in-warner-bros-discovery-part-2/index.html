Fading the Bidding War Narrative in Warner Bros Discovery Part 2

× Date: Mon, Jan 5, 2026

New Developments

Today, January 5, 2026, marks the first full day of regular-way trading in Versant (”VSNT”). Versant is a collection of cable television properties, and some digital properties, that spun out of Comcast. Cable TV is an especially unpopular industry at the moment. By spinning off Versant, Comcast hopes that their remaining businesses will be valued more highly by the stock market. And Versant, a declining but profitable business, can refocus their cash towards their own initiatives instead of diverting it to the growth of Comcast’s other segments like Peacock (streaming).

Why should we care about Versant? Because it’s the best publicly traded comparable to what will be Discovery Global Networks “DGN” (see Part 1 if you’re unfamiliar with DGN). The valuation of DGN matters in determining the overall value of the Netflix merger. Netflix’s mixed consideration–comprising cash, NFLX shares, and DGN equity–will only be deemed superior if its combined value is greater than that of Paramount’s $30 all cash bid. The time value of money matters here as well, which will be considered separately.

At pixel time, VSNT is trading at a price per share of $40 which implies an enterprise value of $8.1B. Versant forecasts ‘26E EBITDA of $1.9B, which is a decrease of 10% from ‘25E EBITDA. Important for our read-through of DGN is the ‘27E EBITDA forecast, which is not provided by VSNT in their investor materials; I assume a further decrease of 10% in 2027.

DGN is expected to spin out of WBD in 3Q26. This means the relevant forecast period for DGN is the 12 months ending 3Q27. VSNT is trading at a 4.6x EV/EBITDA multiple for this time period, based on my estimates.

Applying a 4.6x multiple results in an implied value per share of $1.11 at the midpoint for DGN. Sensitizing it +/- 0.25x results in a low estimate of $0.72 and a high estimate of $1.50.

Above I mentioned the time value of money. In Paramount’s investor materials, they suggest a 6 month delay in closing equates to a decrease in value of $1.25 / share. They expect the Paramount deal will close at least that much sooner than the Netflix deal. You are welcome to quibble with their analysis.

All told, the Paramount deal is superior to the Netflix deal, based on my analysis. The WBD board has not provided their analysis (yet), and at this stage I struggle to see why they raced to sign with Netflix.

It wouldn’t surprise me if Paramount’s analysis is similar. It is possible that they think time is on their side, and by waiting, NFLX stock and VSNT stock will only trade down, further increasing the relative superiority of their $30 all cash bid.

At the very least, this analysis supports my earlier view that Paramount is in no rush to increase their bid.

According to David Faber of CNBC, we should hear back from the WBD board this week regarding their assessment of Paramount’s latest proposal.

Will today’s latest data point: the market value of DGN’s closest comparable, VSNT, be enough to sway the WBD board towards Paramount?
