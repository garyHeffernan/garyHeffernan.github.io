SiriusXM - Not Another One of Those Situations

× Date: Tue, Jun 2, 2026

SiriusXM (“SIRI”) is a high-margin (32% EBITDA margin) levered (approximately 3.6x net debt to EBITDA) cash generative (16% free cash flow margin on elevated capex) satellite radio subscription service with ~33M subscribers and ~255M listeners whose revenue, profits, and subscribers are generally steady but have shown weakness in recent years. I think these shares offer limited downside with the potential for significant upside, depending on how and when its excess spectrum is monetized.

The consensus view is that this company has a satellite-based distribution model that is at risk of becoming obsolete in the face of in-car streaming applications. Any sign of weakness in the subscriber count is pointed to as proof of SiriusXM’s impending obsolescence. Often when I mention this company, the reaction is “why does it need to exist?” and it’s not an unfair question to ask. Apple CarPlay and Android Auto continue to make strides in-vehicle and electric vehicles such as some Teslas are shipped without SiriusXM’s tuner & antenna installed.

Most investors involved in tech, media, and telecom, especially “John Malone” stocks (of which this is one), have seen what happens to businesses when leverage is too high and cash flow shrinks. For example, Charter was caught by surprise by competition at the worst possible time, i.e. when they were carrying too much leverage and increasing their capital intensity to better compete within their ecosystem. Shoot first, ask questions later is a reasonable response to any stock that has a combination of stagnating/shrinking revenue, perceived competition concerns relating to technological obsolescence, and any leverage at all, given recent history. I believe that SiriusXM might be left for dead due to investors simply not wanting to get involved in yet another one of “those situations”.

What’s most interesting about SIRI is that they have already concluded their major satellite capital spending program. This is not Charter where investors are skeptical that capex has peaked. Capex has peaked and there is evidence that it has already started to decline. For example, capex stepped down to $653M due to their most recent satellites having launched in 2024 and 2025 (SiriusXM-9 and SiriusXM-10). The company expects 2026 non-satellite capex in the $400M - $415M range. Satellite capex was $262M in 2024, $204M in 2025, and is expected to be $115M in 2026, $50M in 2027 and near zero in 2028E.

With capex trending lower and revenue stable, SIRI has been busy paying down debt. SIRI’s leverage is already nearing management’s target level. CFO Zac Coughlin said at the JP Morgan Conference in May that by the time SIRI gets into the second half of this year, they will be inside their target leverage range of low to mid threes. Once they are inside their target leverage range they can begin shareholder friendly actions such as share repurchases.

My read is that the company is able to start repurchasing shares if the share price does not increase too much. It’s the kind of catalyst that David Einhorn often talks about: the market isn’t paying attention, or doesn’t care, but it doesn’t matter to the investment proposition. The share repurchases will mechanically increase your proportional ownership in the company. The share price might increase simply from the company purchasing shares in the open market.

If there is no re-rating in the free cash flow or earnings multiple then the stock should still appreciate due to a lower share count, assuming a constant multiple.

This isn’t a high bar because the multiple is so low relative to history (7.5x Forward EV/EBITDA vs. Median of 12.2x, 6.9x P/FCF vs. Median of 15.3x, and 9.2x P/E vs. Median of 19.9x).

I see a business whose revenue is no worse than stable, and cash flow is growing for the next few years. Management points to a business whose revenue line is growing. Revenue has upside potential, separate from member growth and subscription price increases, due to a recently announced Google advertising deal (details are light but the company recently pointed to material growth in total SiriusXM company revenue and material growth in advertising revenue). On why the company needs to exist, the company has historically demonstrated its value proposition for its customers (self-pay monthly churn improved to ~1.5%, the lowest first quarter churn on record) and it is expanding its value proposition by securing rights to live sports, talk shows, and events that aren’t available on other platforms. Finally, the company has the number-one-ranked podcast network per Edison Research. And that is just the operating business.

SIRI also controls valuable spectrum licenses. In total, the company controls 35MHz of contiguous spectrum in the 2GHz band.

I do not anticipate the company liquidating the operating business and monetizing all of its spectrum. But I do believe that the company can monetize 12.5MHz of its excess spectrum that is redundant and was acquired as part of the original Sirius-XM Radio merger. The company has indicated that within 5 years this spectrum could be monetized. I believe a conservative floor valuation is $1/MHz-POP for this asset or $4.19B or ~$12.5/share in value on today’s share count. In the last 12 months, mid-band spectrum transactions have commanded valuations >$1/MHz-POP (Echostar/AT&T @ $1.37-$1.40, EchoStar/SpaceX @ $1.01).

To believe my valuation, and even to think that it might be too low, you have to imagine a world where “new telcos” look more akin to SpaceX’s Starlink where the connectivity is driven by satellite infrastructure vs. traditional landed telco infrastructure (e.g. cell towers / antennas that densify terrestrial networks). If that proves to be where the industry is headed, then the traditional knock on SIRI’s spectrum – that it’s “downlink only” or “one-way” and deserves a discount – will no longer hold. Anyone interested in building this new, space-based radio access network e.g. AT&T, SpaceX, Amazon, and others, won’t apply a discount to the SDARS band due to its downlink-only nature. Downlink spectrum is in short supply, and a nationwide contiguous band from one licensee will likely command an auction among multiple bidding parties. An auction of a scarce asset can produce surprisingly positive results. As well, SIRI is not a distressed seller, and is in no rush to sell if the price isn’t to their liking. In my view this represents a source of upside that is not priced into the shares today, but you do have to be patient to let it play out. Anyone who invested in DISH (now SATS) a decade ago knows just how long these monetization events can take. A recent piece from Bloomberg Intelligence corroborates my view that $1/MHz-POP represents a floor; they suggest the spectrum could transact at $1.50/MHz-POP, or ~$18.75/share in value on today’s share count.

Trigger Events

One trigger event to make money is the resumption of share repurchases. The company authorized a $1.2B share repurchase program which has $1B remaining unused at FY25YE. The company is guiding to the resumption of share repurchases as early as the second half of this year. If the stock doesn’t mechanically go up, you’ll still own a larger percentage of the business, but my suspicion is that the stock will rise. My view, which mirrors Einhorn’s, is that there aren’t as many fundamental investors contributing to price discovery in the current market environment. This leads to shares remaining undervalued for extended periods of time if there is no catalyst. The company themselves can serve as that catalyst by repurchasing shares. That’s what I expect to happen here. If history is any guide, almost all of FCF can be directed towards share repurchases. If the company did that in ‘27 and ‘28 it would reduce the share count by about a third, assuming no change in the share price. This would be a welcome result for shareholders if the company is not in terminal decline. And the math starts to get very interesting if a spectrum monetization occurs after a significant reduction in the share count.

Another trigger event is simply investor appreciation of the Direct-to-Device opportunity, and the spectrum required to build out a space-based radio access network. The SpaceX S-1 already touches on this, and the SpaceX IPO itself should draw a lot of investor attention to this area. From the SpaceX S-1:

“Even if the transaction is completed, there can be no assurance that our purchase of licenses from EchoStar will be sufficient to meet our growing need for spectrum licenses and we may be unable to find other parties to provide us with additional spectrum licenses on terms acceptable to us, or at all. We may in the future pursue additional acquisitions, leases, or other arrangements relating to spectrum rights in order to support the expansion of our connectivity services, and there can be no assurance that we will be able to enter into or complete any such transactions or arrangements on acceptable terms, or at all.” As the company releases more details on the advertising opportunity, there is the potential for the shares to appreciate as investors model revenue growth for this company. More information will be forthcoming. If it does translate to total company revenue growth as the company has recently indicated then the shares should re-rate higher since the company will no longer be perceived as a stagnant / melting ice-cube.

Finally, the actual monetization of the excess 12.5MHz of spectrum, which the company suggested could occur within five years’ time, will be another trigger event. Further, it doesn’t have to be a space-based acquirer. The threat from Starlink to traditional telcos could make a spectrum acquisition by a telco a defensive purchase to block Starlink’s market access.

Recent Events and Risks

On May 29, 2026, the NYT reported that SIRI and iHeartRadio (IHRT) put their merger talks on ice. If the talks resume then it muddies my thesis (e.g. I would expect excess cash to go towards M&A vs. share repurchases), but I don’t think it kills the idea. A combined SIRI/IHRT could be an interesting combination, depending on the strategy.

On March 26, 2026, the FCC adopted a NPRM for “Spectrum Abundance for Weird Space Stuff”(SB Docket 26-54; FCC 26-13). The proposal is for a new secondary non-federal Space Operation Service (SOS) allocation in 2320–2345 MHz (Earth-to-space direction) which is SIRI’s SDARS band. Under the proposal SIRI would act as exclusive licensee and lease the spectrum to earth station operators for satellite telemetry, tracking & command in the in- space servicing, assembly, and manufacturing economy. This is an option for SIRI should they not sell the spectrum, but that economy is nascent at the moment which makes it hard for me to place a value on the option.

Obvious risks are that the subscriber count enters a rapid decline, churn increases, ARPU shrinks to retain share, and the spectrum is not monetized.

Catalyst

  • Resumption of share repurchase program
  • Investor appreciation of and monetization of spectrum opportunity
  • Investor appreciation of and revenue growth from ads opportunity
