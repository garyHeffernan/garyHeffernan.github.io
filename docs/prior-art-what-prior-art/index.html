Prior art? What prior art?

$LQDA Part IV

× Date: Tue, Nov 8, 2022

The immediate path forward for Liquidia ($LQDA) comes down to what qualifies as prior art. I think Liquidia is exceptionally well-positioned here based on what we have learned from the PTAB’s Final Written Decision. Remember, I’m an investor, not a lawyer, and it wouldn’t be the first time I’ve gotten something wrong. Do your own work!

Unsatisfied with my prior post “Someone is wrong on the internet”, I decided to dig a little more into the merits of United Therapeutic (UT)’s request for rehearing of the PTAB case. Recall what UT argued in the original PTAB case:

  1. Liquidia fails to show that JESC and JAHA are prior art to the ’793 patent
  2. Liquidia fails to show that the combination of the ‘212 patent, JESC and JAHA teaches or suggests all the limitations of any of the challenged claims.
  3. Liquidia fails to show that a Person of Ordinary Skill in the Art (POSA) would have had a reason to combine the teachings of these references.

(JESC? JAHA? If what you just read doesn’t make sense, please go back to the beginning and read Parts One, Two, and Three).

Also recall what the PTAB decided in their Final Written Decision (FWD): the PTAB found all three claims in Liquidia’s favor: 1) JESC and JAHA are prior art to the ‘793 patent, 2) the combination of ‘212, JESC, and JAHA teaches or suggests all the limitations of any of the challenged claims, and 3) a POSA would have had a reason to combine the teachings of these references.

Because the PTAB ruled in Liquidia’s favor, they found the ‘793 patent invalid. If and when the PTAB’s decision is affirmed on appeal by the US Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit (C.A.F.C.), the ‘793 patent will be canceled, Liquidia will receive final FDA approval for and launch Yutrepia.

Prior to being heard by the C.A.F.C., the PTAB will decide whether to rehear the original case. If they don’t rehear the original case, the C.A.F.C. will hear the appeal. Recall, the C.A.F.C. has sided with the PTAB on ~73% of decisions, so what I believe is the most important thing in the event path, as it stands today, is whether or not Liquidia is likely to win at the PTAB.

In UT’s request for rehearing at the PTAB, they only dispute one of the PTAB’s findings in the FWD. They do not contest points 2 and 3 above. They only contest point one, which is whether or not JESC & JAHA are prior art. In a way, this makes our analysis much simpler since we only have to focus on the key question: does the PTAB think JESC and JAHA qualify as prior art?

Here’s what the PTAB said in their FWD regarding the prior art status of the JESC and JAHA references (“the references”):

“But Petitioner does not rely solely on availability in libraries to show the prior-art status of Voswinckel JESC and Voswinckel JAHA. Instead, Petitioner also argues that “Voswinckel JESC is an abstract presented at the European Society of Cardiology (JESC) Congress,” that Voswinckel JAHA “was publicly presented at the 2004 Scientific Sessions of the American Heart Association,” and that both references were cited in other documents dating from before the priority date of the ’793 patent whose public accessibility is not at issue.”

In the above excerpt from the FWD, the PTAB states that Liquidia made three arguments that support the references’ prior-art status: A) JESC was publicly presented at the European Society of Cardiology Congress, B) JAHA was publicly presented at the 2004 Scientific Sessions of the American Heart Association and C) both references were cited in other documents.

Here is what I believe to be the biggest clue in this back and forth between UT and the PTAB. In UT’s request for rehearing, they contest point C: they say that the references are not prior art because the way they were cited in other documents is invalid (and they might be right about this, but it’s irrelevant in my opinion). But they do not contest points A and B! They say nothing about the fact that the references were publicly presented at conferences. Why am I so confident on the importance of this clue? Let’s go back to the PTAB’s FWD:

“As to both Voswinckel JESC and Voswinckel JAHA, Petitioner’s Reply evidence showing citation to the references in other publicly accessible documents [Clay: this is point C above] is merely additional evidence supporting Petitioner’s original theory that a person of ordinary skill in the art could have located the references.”

The PTAB is saying that public accessibility in other documents — the very thing that UT is contesting — is “merely additional evidence” which supports Liquidia’s claim that a POSA could have located the references. Remove the mere additional evidence from the picture (which is UT’s sole basis for the rehearing request) and a POSA still could have located the references. And if a POSA still could have located the references, then the references are prior art. And if the references are prior art, the ‘793 is invalid. This seems cut and dry to me.

The PTAB has been given an opportunity to clarify their thinking on this matter by the PTAB’s Precedential Opinion Panel (POP). The POP read the FWD at UT’s request and noticed that the PTAB’s support of their opinion was light; the PTAB simply stated their view without supporting analysis (see POP decision here). It is my view that the PTAB will come back and clarify their view on this matter, effectively shutting the door in UT’s face with regards to the rehearing request.

Agree? Disagree? Please let me know in the comments. And don’t be shy about sharing with your Patent Lawyer friends. The more eyes on this, the better!

Side note 1: a reasonable question might be: to what end is UT pursuing this legal strategy? My view is that this legal strategy is consistent with drawing out the clock as long as possible to keep Liquidia’s Yutrepia out of the marketplace. My suspicion is corroborated by UT running that playbook — with the same lawyer from the same law firm — in their attempt to squash competitor SteadyMed. In the case of SteadyMed, UT took them to court for as long as possible. Once it was clear that UT would lose, they decided to acquire SteadyMed.

Side note 2 : @TheIPHawk on Twitter thinks the POP helped Liquidia because once the PTAB concretely lays out their reasoning on the prior art issue, it is less likely to be questioned by the C.A.F.C. during the appeal. His view is that we will get a rehearing decision by the PTAB between 3-6 weeks from 10/27/2022.
