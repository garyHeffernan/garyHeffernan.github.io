What Versant implies about the price of legacy TV

× Date: Thu, Feb 19, 2026

Investment Thesis

With the conclusion of the Netflix / Paramount / Warner Bros Discovery bidding war on the horizon, we are about to see a transaction comparable print for Versant. My hypothesis is that the transaction comparable is likely to come in at 5x EV/EBITDA, well above where VSNT trades in the public market today. While I do not expect an imminent re-rating to this price, nor do I expect an imminent take-private in Versant, the transaction comparable should highlight to investors the value of Legacy TV assets. Said another way, Versant’s current price reflects a purchase price that is attractive when measured against the yardstick of value to a private owner. The stock trades at 3.1x ’25 EV/EBITDA and 3.1x ’25 FCF today and has a lightly levered balance sheet at 1.1x net leverage. This valuation margin of safety gives the investment staying power as we wait for a take-out.

Company Overview

Versant Media Group (“VSNT”) is a “house of brands” mass media company, recently spun off from Comcast, that manages a diverse portfolio of legacy cable networks including USA Network, CNBC, and MSNBC. The company also holds valuable digital assets such as a majority stake in Fandango Media, Rotten Tomatoes, and various sports technology platforms.

The Transaction Comparable

What follows is a bit of a variant perception on the Netflix / Paramount / Warner Bros Discovery bidding war. From this variant perception, we can draw a conclusion about the valuation of Legacy TV assets, of which Versant is the closest publicly traded comparable and hence the subject of this writeup. I think it is well-founded, but like any good variant perception, it will probably be vehemently disagreed with because it conflicts with what we have all read in the press despite being consistent with company filings (not press reports and trade rumors), company actions and incentives. Since this is a variant perception, I do not think it is reflected in VSNT’s price at all, and therefore poses little downside risk to the shares should it not materialize. Heads I win, tails I don’t lose (much).

Discovery Global Networks (“DG”) is Versant’s closest comparable, and is about to be sold. Warner Bros Discovery (“WBD”) signed a merger agreement with Netflix to sell their studios and streaming business, excluding DG, for $27.75 per share. Paramount bid $30 per share for the entirety of WBD, putting an implied valuation of $2.25 per share on DG. WBD rejected Paramount’s bid. Subsequently, WBD put out their own valuation of DG at $4.63-$6.86 per share. Paramount volleyed back with a verbal bid from their banker at $31 for the entire company, which implies they’re raising their bid for DG to $3.25 per share. The WBD board said they expect a higher price than that. I think a base case is that we land at $4/share for DG, or 5x EBITDA. My view is that this price is arrived at as early as Monday, February 23, when Paramount’s special waiver to negotiate with the board of WBD expires.

WBD’s Board thinks DG is worth between $4.63-$6.86 per share:

The overall low to high latest 12 months adjusted EBITDA multiples observed for the selected diversified transactions were 7.9x to 16.5x, the overall low to high latest 12 months adjusted EBITDA multiples observed for the selected U.S. linear transactions were 6.5x to 11.2x and the latest 12 months adjusted EBITDA multiples observed for the selected international linear transactions were 7.0x to 8.7x. Allen & Company and J.P. Morgan applied a selected range of latest 12 months adjusted EBITDA multiples derived from the selected transactions of 5.5x to 6.5x to the latest 12 months adjusted EBITDA (as of December 31, 2025) of Discovery Global, which indicated an approximate implied equity value reference range for Discovery Global of $4.63 to $6.86 per share.

WBD’s Board did not exercise their fiduciary out, but thinks the sum-of-the-parts is worth more than $31 per share:

We are writing to inform you that Netflix has agreed to provide WBD a waiver of certain terms of the Netflix merger agreement to permit us, through February 23, to engage with PSKY to clarify your proposal, which we understand will include a WBD per share price higher than $31. We seek your best and final proposal. To be clear, our Board has not determined that your proposal is reasonably likely to result in a transaction that is superior to the Netflix merger. We continue to recommend and remain fully committed to our transaction with Netflix and have scheduled a special meeting of our shareholders on March 20, 2026 to vote on the Netflix merger agreement.

My read of the situation is that the WBD board has not re-opened merger talks for WB streaming and studios, on account of a signed merger agreement with Netflix. The way the board would re-open the bidding is to exercise their fiduciary out, which they have explicitly not done (“To be clear, our Board has not determined that your proposal is reasonably likely to result in a transaction that is superior to the Netflix merger.”). The fiduciary out exists to allow the board to exercise their fiduciary duty to shareholders in the event a superior transaction materializes or is likely to materialize. The board explicitly said that is not what occurred with Paramount’s proposal.

Since they have not exercised their fiduciary out, you’d have to believe that Netflix re-opened the bidding process. I cannot think of any reason that a buyer would agree to re-open a bidding process after they already won. Recall, Netflix was going to withdraw their offer if WBD didn’t go exclusive and accept their terms before market open the morning following the receipt of the final bid.

To beat this point to death, look at the recent contest for Metsera to see what I mean. Metsera exercised their fiduciary out, and the buyer, Pfizer, was so upset that they sued Metsera for breach of contract, breach of fiduciary duty, and tortious interference. Which is what you’d expect a buyer to do if they were about to lose a signed deal. Metsera was right in exercising their out because it was that action that caused a bidding war. We don’t have that here in Netflix / Paramount / Warner Bros Discovery.

Since the spin-off of DG is a closing condition of the merger, it is in WBD’s and NFLX’s mutual interest to get Paramount to stop making so much noise and settle on a price for DG. And that’s what I think is happening here. I think Netflix let Paramount under the tent to finally agree upon a price for DG. Then, when the shareholder vote comes on March 20, WBD shareholders will know exactly what they are voting on, as a merger agreement for DG will remove the debt reallocation mechanism that threatens to reduce the cash consideration in the Netflix merger.

If you disagree with my variant perception, and think that we are heading into a bidding war between Netflix and Paramount for WBD, then I think that situation does not argue for a lower valuation for DG than what I have argued so far.

Valuation

In 2025, Versant did $6.6bn in Revenue, $2.2bn in Adj. EBITDA, and $1.4bn in FCF. The stock trades at a 3.1x ’25 EV/EBITDA multiple and 3.1x ’25 P/FCF multiple. The valuation is low, likely due to the company being in a hated sector (legacy TV) and being a newly formed company, as it was a spin-off that completed in early January of this year.

At 5x EBITDA, the stock has ~97% upside. It’s logical to think that Comcast would prefer to spin Versant before selling it to a strategic or private equity buyer, effectively a disguised sale.

Risks

As it stands today, this is a business whose financial profile is dominated by Linear TV. And we all know that Linear TV is in decline. In 2024, 17% of revenue was from non-pay TV (platforms, digital advertising, and other). VSNT’s 3-5 year goal is to shift this mix to ~33%, and their long-term goal is to reach ~50%. I don’t have a strong view if they are likely to achieve their stated goals given the newness of this company and limited track record. Should they not achieve their goals, absent a stabilization in linear TV, we’re looking at a melting ice cube.

Another risk to this thesis is that Brian Roberts maintains ~33% voting control in Versant through his high-vote Class B shares. Roberts has historically not been a seller, and could stand in the way of any sale. Versant also has growth aspirations in digital, and could poorly invest excess capital at the expense of shareholders. I think this risk is somewhat mitigated by the presence of David Novak as chairman of the board, the impressive former CEO of Yum! Brands. Further, when it was a part of Comcast, VSNT was the “cash cow” that funded Comcast’s buildout of Peacock. VSNT no longer serves that purpose, its cash flow is its own, and to me that suggests that Roberts might be willing to let it go.

Another risk is that Paramount and Warner Bros Discovery do not come to an agreement on the value of DG. My view is that Paramount has been making so much noise precisely because they want to force the issue of a sale before DG is allowed to spin-off. My view is that they want to reduce competition in acquiring this asset. So, if Paramount walks, then I’d expect other buyers to materialize for DG and VSNT in the future.

Another risk to this thesis lies in the assumption of the debt allocation in DG and what it implies for a transaction multiple. As it stands today, the WBD board expects to allocate $17bn in debt to DG. With this allocation of debt, their valuation of DG is $4.63-$6.86 per share, or 5.5x-6.5x LTM EBITDA. It is not given that DG’s capital structure will have $17bn in debt, as it could shift back to streaming and studios. One reason I believe that DG will retain $17bn in debt is that the Co-CEO of Netflix, Ted Sarandos, as recently as yesterday, is adamant that shareholders in the Netflix / WB merger will receive $27.75 in cash, i.e. the debt reallocation mechanism will not be triggered to reduce the Netflix merger consideration.

A final risk to this thesis is the merger consideration for DG. If it’s all-stock, the stock may ultimately trade poorly, and the 5x EBITDA transaction multiple that I’m expecting could prove to be a mirage.
